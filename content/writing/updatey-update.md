Well.

The moped has broken again.

I would like to say I'm surprised, but at this point that would be dishonest.

Last time I wrote about it, it was finally working. After months of chasing charging problems, waiting for parts from overseas, and generally becoming far too familiar with the electrical system of a fifty cubic centimetre engine, I was actually getting somewhere.

And for a while, it was great. I rode it for ages. It started when it was supposed to, went where I wanted it to go, and did the one thing I'd bought it for in the first place: transport me from one place to another without requiring a small engineering project beforehand.

Then, quite suddenly, it lost power.

The clutch had completely burned out.

Which is less than ideal, obviously.

The really lovely part is the smell. Everything smells like burnt clutch now, which is remarkably fish-like. The sort of smell that makes you check whether something has gone off in the fridge, only to remember that the problem is, in fact, your entire vehicle.

I hate this thing.

The replacement part is also an absolutely ridiculous size, which makes finding one an exercise in frustration. It's not exactly a complicated component, but apparently it exists in some deeply inconvenient combination of dimensions that means I can't simply order a replacement and get on with my life.

So here we are again. Another problem, another search for parts, and another reminder that buying a cheap moped is a very effective way to spend a surprising amount of time not actually having a working moped.

I suppose I'm learning things. I would quite like to learn them on something else.

In other news, I'm selling the MacBook.

The five-dollar MacBook Pro was a fun project. There is something deeply satisfying about buying a water-damaged laptop for less than the price of lunch, replacing a couple of components, and getting it to boot again. I still think that was a pretty good result.

But having a working computer and actually wanting to use it are two quite different things.

I don't particularly like the MacBook. I gave it a fair go, and I can appreciate the engineering that went into it, but I keep finding myself wishing I had my old laptop back. So, naturally, I'm going back to the ThinkPad X1 Nano.

The one with the BIOS problem.

Which I fixed.

There's something comforting about returning to a machine you've already repaired. You know its quirks, you know what you've done to it, and you know that when something inevitably goes wrong, there's at least a reasonable chance you'll be able to work out why. It's familiar in a way that a new machine never quite is.

Besides, it's a ThinkPad. I'm not sure I ever stood a chance of staying away.

The phone situation is heading in much the same direction.

My Pixel 5 is hanging on by a thread. It's been a good little phone, considering it was released in 2020, but I think we're approaching the point where optimism is no longer a viable maintenance strategy.

Ideally, I'd like a Samsung Galaxy S25.

We like Samsung here.

There's something to be said for a phone that feels properly polished, has hardware I actually want to use, and doesn't require me to make excuses for it every other week. The Pixel has served me well enough, but I'd quite like something newer, faster, and a little less likely to become a project in its own right.

Unfortunately, between the moped, the computer situation, and the general cost of being alive, I suspect the S25 may have to remain a goal for a little while longer.

Which is probably sensible. Annoyingly.

And Bella is Bella.

There's not much more to report there, really. She's still here, still putting up with me, and still being herself. Sometimes I feel like I should have some grand update to offer, some new development or revelation, but not everything needs to change to be worth writing about.

It's nice to have something that doesn't need fixing.

Especially lately.

I suppose that's where things are at the moment. The moped is broken, the MacBook is going, the ThinkPad is coming back, and the Pixel is living on borrowed time. Nothing particularly dramatic, just the usual collection of little projects and inconveniences that somehow manage to occupy an entire month.

I keep thinking I'll get everything sorted out and have a nice, quiet stretch where nothing breaks and I can simply enjoy things for a bit.

This has yet to happen.

Maybe next month.

With love,

Daniel

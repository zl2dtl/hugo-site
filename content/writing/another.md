Another late night blog post after a very stressful day.

I guess the blog is now being used like my old one was again.

Something that scared me more today than what I was talking about yesterday: losing my laptop.

I thought it was about to go bad earlier because the keyboard was playing up, the trackpad was scrolling weirdly, and suddenly the whole thing was just a mess. This is exactly why I don't let anyone else use it if I have the choice. Probably slightly irrational, but I know how I treat my things, and I know how other people treat theirs.

So that scared me.

Maybe for similar reasons, though.

It's about losing something I've worked so hard for. One is tangible and one isn't quantifiable in any way, but the fear feels strangely similar.

This laptop has been the thing I've relied on most. For writing, for thinking, for creating, for fixing things, for wasting time, for learning things I probably didn't need to learn. It's been there for an absurd amount of my life over the last few years.

And there's a story within it in and of itself.

I didn't just buy this laptop. I found it water-damaged for five dollars, opened it up, repaired the corrosion, replaced things that needed replacing, and somehow ended up with the machine I use every day. There are memories attached to it that have nothing to do with the actual hardware.

That's what I think I'm attached to.

Not the laptop itself. More the memories and the concepts it holds.

I like things with stories. I like hearing the stories behind things. A thing that has been somewhere, belonged to someone, been repaired, broken, modified or carried around for years is infinitely more interesting to me than something that just came out of a box yesterday.

Nothing really exists independently.

Even this laptop is just a collection of materials that came from somewhere, assembled by thousands of people I've never met, passed through other hands, damaged, repaired and eventually ended up with me. The story didn't start when I got it, and it won't necessarily end when I stop using it.

And I suppose people aren't really any different.

We're all connected to billions of other people, whether we know them or not, all sitting on this floating hunk of rock positioned rather conveniently between Venus and Mars, at a temperature that happens to allow life to exist.

Which, when you think about it, is completely ridiculous.

Somewhere billions of years ago, stars exploded and scattered the elements that would eventually become everything around us. Then somehow you get carbon. Then bacteria. Then plants. Then dinosaurs. Then people.

And now I'm sitting in bed at nearly eleven at night worrying about my laptop and trying to understand my own life.

Tada! Carbon!

That's one hell of a tangent.

I think I spend my whole life looking at tiny components under a microscope.

I used to spend too much time overanalysing everything, so I zoomed in. I thought that if I looked closely enough, I could understand things properly. Except after doing that, I somehow ended up spending the same amount of time overanalysing things, except now I have a much smaller sample size to make generalisations from.

Maybe that's what I've been doing with everything else too.

Zooming in so far that I forget there's a world outside whatever tiny thing I'm currently examining.

And I think that's part of why I don't share my thoughts.

It's not necessarily that I don't want to. I just don't know if I'm fully able to understand most of what I think myself, even usually. I think things that I haven't fully thought through, and I don't share them because I don't fully understand them.

Some things make sense to me.

Some things make absolutely no sense to me.

And some things make less sense the more time I spend thinking about them.

Then there are the thoughts that aren't even entirely mine to begin with. Sometimes someone can say something enough times, or badger you about something for long enough, that eventually it gets planted in your head. You start thinking about it because you've been told to think about it, and eventually you can't think about anything else.

And that's not good.

So perhaps keeping everything in my head isn't as simple as being selfish, either.

Maybe sometimes I don't share because I don't have anything coherent to share. It's just a collection of half-formed ideas, worries, assumptions, memories and things other people have said, all bouncing around in my head and occasionally colliding with each other.

But I suppose that's what thinking is.

You don't always start with an answer. Sometimes you don't even start with a question.

Sometimes you just start with something that won't leave you alone.

Someone once said to me, "The only person that can control your mind is you."

I've thought about that a lot today.

When people go wrong, things can't necessarily just be fixed. But people things are caused by people. Breaking a phone is technically a people thing too, but sometimes you can just blame the universe for putting gravity there. Being completely ignorant to something because you never considered it, though? That's a people thing. That's something you can blame on the person.

And maybe that's where I have some control.

I can't control everything that happens, or what other people think, or what the universe decides to throw at me. But I can decide what I do with it. I can decide whether I keep staring at the tiny component under the microscope, or whether I occasionally zoom back out and remember there's an entire circuit board around it.

I was thinking about all of this earlier when my watch decided to tell me that my heart rate was high.

Which was helpful.

Why bother with an expensive device that just annoys you constantly?

Anyway, I think I'm slowly getting better at zooming out.

Not everything needs to be solved immediately. Not every thought needs an answer. Some things probably need to be said, some things need more thinking, and some things might just need to be left alone.

The laptop seems to be fine, by the way.

Which is probably good, because I have enough things to worry about without adding "my entire digital existence has just died" to the list.

Things break. People change. Plans go wrong. Sometimes you can fix things, sometimes you can't, and sometimes you realise that the thing you were so worried about was never actually happening in the first place.

I don't know.

Maybe that's okay.

I'm still looking through the microscope. I'm just trying to remember to look up occasionally.

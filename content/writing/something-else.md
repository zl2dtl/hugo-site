Another month, another post.

Following another week of relentless pestering to write something for this blog, I've finally decided to sit down and do it. Not because I've had some particularly profound realisation, or because anything especially interesting has happened, but apparently people expect me to keep this thing updated. Fair enough, I suppose.

It's been a fairly standard month, all things considered. Studying, working, and generally trying to keep on top of everything. There have been a few purchases, a few projects, and the usual collection of things that seemed like good ideas at the time.

Most importantly, I've bought a new phone.

After what feels like an unnecessarily long period of deliberation, I've eventually settled on a Samsung Galaxy S25. I spent quite a while comparing options, reading specifications, and convincing myself that I was making a thoroughly sensible and considered purchase. In the end, I wanted something with a decent amount of software support left, good hardware, and, perhaps most importantly, a microphone that actually works.

The Pixel 5 had served me reasonably well, but its microphone had developed a rather unfortunate habit of intermittently cutting out during calls. Much to the discontent of anyone unfortunate enough to speak to me. It's difficult to have a conversation when the other person can only hear every third sentence. The S25 is still on its way, but I'm looking forward to having a phone that can manage the fairly basic task of being a phone.

I've also been spending some time working on a rather lovely piece of test equipment: the Hewlett-Packard 34401A Digital Multimeter.

It's a beautiful bit of kit. A proper six-and-a-half-digit bench multimeter, built for making accurate measurements rather than simply displaying a number and hoping for the best. It has the sort of construction that makes you appreciate how much thought went into equipment of this era. The manuals are wonderfully written, the interface is sensible, and even the silicone test probes are an absolute pleasure to use. There is something quite special about equipment that feels like it was designed by people who genuinely cared about the person using it.

Unfortunately, it has a problem. The ancient tantalum capacitors have started failing, with a couple having gone rather spectacularly pop leaving my room more smokey than a school bathroom and also leaving my shirt withy an unfortunate stench of burning electronics. They've scorched the circuit board in the process, leaving behind a rather unpleasant amount of carbonised material. Which brings me to my current activity: carefully scraping away all the old carbon without accidentally removing any of the copper pads that are still perfectly usable.

It's a wonderfully delicate job. Carbonised PCB material can be conductive, so simply leaving it there isn't really an option. But the board is old, the damage is localised, and I would quite like to avoid turning a repair into a much larger problem by being too enthusiastic with a scraping tool. The aim is to remove the damaged material, clean everything up, inspect the remaining tracks and pads, and replace the failed components.

Still, I think it's worth the effort. The 34401A is a serious instrument, with excellent measurement capabilities and a level of build quality that makes it difficult not to admire. It would be a shame to let a handful of ageing components put an end to something that was clearly built to last.

On a slightly less exciting note, I've also finally invested in a proper screwdriver set: the iFixit Pro Tech Toolkit. I've been meaning to buy one for ages, and now that they're actually available from a shop in the country, I could no longer justify putting it off. It's a lovely set. Everything has its place, the bits are comprehensive, and the whole thing feels considerably more organised than the collection of random screwdrivers and bits I've accumulated over the years. I love it already, which is probably a slightly unreasonable amount of affection to have for a screwdriver set, but here we are.

Speaking of things I love, Bella is still very much Bella.  We've somehow developed a habit of communicating in increasingly broken French. I'm not entirely sure how we got here, but it's become one of those little things that makes me more happy than it likely should, both amusement and awe. Je t'aime, mon amour. 

And I think that's probably enough of an update for one month. I've got a multimeter to repair, a growing pile of things to study, a phone somewhere in the postal system, and a rather nice new screwdriver set to find excuses to use. Life continues, mostly in the form of small projects, occasional purchases, and trying to find enough hours in the day to do everything I'd like to.

Nothing particularly extraordinary, but I suppose that's the point. Not every month needs to be memorable. Sometimes it's enough to have things to work on, people to love, and something to look forward to.

I'll try not to make everyone wait quite so long for the next one, though that being said it's likely to take a while.

With love,

Daniel
